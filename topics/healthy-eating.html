Food is Fuel and Medicine

What you eat affects how you feel, think, and function. Food provides energy and the building blocks your body needs to work properly.

Good nutrition can prevent disease and help you recover faster from illness.

How Digestion Works

When you eat:

  1. Your mouth: Saliva breaks down food as you chew
  2. Your stomach: Acid and enzymes continue breaking down food
  3. Your small intestine: Nutrients are absorbed into your bloodstream
  4. Your large intestine: Water is absorbed; waste becomes stool
  5. Elimination: Your body removes what it doesn't need

How Food Becomes Energy

Your body converts food into glucose (blood sugar) and uses it for energy:

  • Carbohydrates: Quickest energy source (sugars and starches)
  • Proteins: Build and repair muscles and tissues
  • Fats: Long-lasting energy, protect organs
  • Vitamins & minerals: Help your body use energy and stay healthy
  • Fiber: Helps digestion, blood sugar control, heart health

Blood Sugar and Energy

What you eat affects your blood sugar and energy levels:

Fast Carbs = Quick Energy Spike (then crash)

  • White bread, pastries, candy, sugary drinks
  • Quick rise in blood sugar and energy
  • Quick drop, leaving you tired and hungry

Complex Carbs = Steady Energy (no crash)

  • Whole grains, beans, vegetables, fruits
  • Slower rise in blood sugar
  • Steady, sustained energy
  • Stay full longer

How Food Affects Your Mood

Food influences brain chemicals that affect mood:

  • Good nutrition: Improves mood, reduces anxiety and depression
  • Protein and healthy fats: Support brain health
  • B vitamins: Help with mood and energy
  • Sugar crashes: Can worsen mood and anxiety
  • Regular meals: Prevent mood swings from low blood sugar

Food and Sleep

What and when you eat affects sleep quality:

  • Heavy meals: Eat 2-3 hours before bed
  • Caffeine: Avoid 6+ hours before sleep
  • Alcohol: May help you fall asleep but disrupts sleep quality
  • Large amounts of water: Can disturb sleep with bathroom trips
  • Protein and complex carbs: Can promote better sleep

Food and Inflammation

Some foods increase inflammation; others reduce it:

Foods That Increase Inflammation

  • Processed foods
  • Foods high in added sugar
  • Trans fats
  • Too much omega-6 (vegetable oils)

Foods That Reduce Inflammation

  • Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel)
  • Berries
  • Leafy greens
  • Nuts and seeds
  • Olive oil
  • Tomatoes
  • Spices (turmeric, ginger)

Food and Chronic Disease Prevention

Good nutrition can prevent or delay:

  • Type 2 diabetes - limit sugar and refined carbs
  • Heart disease - choose lean proteins and healthy fats
  • High blood pressure - reduce sodium, increase potassium
  • Certain cancers - eat plenty of vegetables and fruits
  • Obesity - maintain healthy portions and balanced nutrition

Hydration Matters

Water is essential for:

  • Digestion
  • Temperature regulation
  • Joint lubrication
  • Brain function and mood
  • Nutrient absorption
  • Waste removal

Goal: 8-10 glasses of water per day, or more in hot weather or with exercise

Practical Tips

  • Eat regular meals to maintain steady blood sugar
  • Choose whole foods over processed
  • Eat the rainbow (colorful vegetables and fruits)
  • Include protein at every meal
  • Drink water instead of sugary drinks
  • Pay attention to how foods make you feel
  • Don't skip meals - it affects energy and mood
  • Plan meals to avoid poor choices when hungry
