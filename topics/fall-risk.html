Fall-Risk Assessment

Why Falls Matter

Falls are the leading cause of injury for older adults. One fall can change your life. Many seniors never fully recover from a serious fall.

The good news: Most falls can be prevented. Understanding your fall risk is the first step.

Are You at Risk for Falls?

You are at higher risk if:

  • You have fallen in the past
  • You have balance or walking problems
  • You have weak legs or feet
  • You take multiple medications
  • You have low blood pressure (dizziness when standing)
  • You have vision or hearing problems
  • You have arthritis or joint pain
  • You are over 65
  • You live alone
  • Your home has hazards (loose rugs, poor lighting, no grab bars)

Common Fall Hazards at Home

Hazard How to Fix It
Loose or curled rugs Tape down or remove them
Clutter or cords Clear pathways, tape down cords
Poor lighting Add lights, especially on stairs
Wet or slippery floors Use non-slip mats, clean spills quickly
Stairs without handrails Install sturdy handrails on both sides
Bathrooms without grab bars Install grab bars near toilet and shower
Shoes without grip Wear slip-resistant shoes

What To Do

Prevent Falls

  • Exercise: Walking, tai chi, and balance exercises strengthen muscles
  • Wear proper shoes: Non-slip, flat shoes with good support
  • Use assistive devices: Cane, walker, or grab bars if needed
  • Have your eyes checked: Good vision helps prevent falls
  • Review medications: Some can cause dizziness
  • Check vitamin D: Low levels weaken bones
  • Make home changes: See table above

If You Fall

  • Don't panic
  • Call for help if possible
  • Move slowly and carefully
  • Roll onto your side, then onto hands and knees
  • Crawl to a sturdy chair and use it to stand
  • Sit and rest before moving
  • Check for injuries

When to Seek Help

  • You've had a fall
  • You feel unsteady or fear falling
  • You have pain after a fall
  • You've noticed changes in balance or walking
  • You have frequent near-falls

Home Safety Checklist

Complete This Checklist

  • ☐ Remove tripping hazards (rugs, cords, clutter)
  • ☐ Improve lighting throughout home
  • ☐ Install handrails on stairs
  • ☐ Add grab bars in bathroom
  • ☐ Secure slippery rugs
  • ☐ Wear sturdy, non-slip shoes indoors
  • ☐ Keep frequently used items at arm level
  • ☐ Keep a phone nearby at all times
  • ☐ Consider a medical alert system

Medical Alert Systems

If you live alone, consider a medical alert system. You can press a button to call for help if you fall.

  • Wearable devices (watch, pendant)
  • Home-based systems
  • Mobile phone apps
  • Many are affordable or free for seniors

Ask your doctor or local senior center about options.
