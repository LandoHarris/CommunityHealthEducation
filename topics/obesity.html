Understanding Weight and Health

Maintaining a healthy weight is important for your overall health. Obesity increases risk for many serious diseases.

However, weight is just one part of health. You can be healthy at different sizes, and many people need support to manage their weight.

What is Obesity?

Obesity is determined by BMI (Body Mass Index), which relates height to weight.

  • Underweight: BMI less than 18.5
  • Normal weight: BMI 18.5 - 24.9
  • Overweight: BMI 25 - 29.9
  • Obese: BMI 30 or higher

Calculate your BMI: Visit CDC BMI Calculator

Health Risks of Obesity

Excess weight increases risk for:

  • Type 2 diabetes
  • Heart disease and high blood pressure
  • Stroke
  • Certain cancers
  • Sleep apnea
  • Fatty liver disease
  • Joint problems and arthritis
  • Depression and anxiety

Causes of Weight Gain

  • Eating more calories than you burn
  • Lack of physical activity
  • Genetics (family history)
  • Medical conditions (thyroid, PCOS, depression)
  • Medications
  • Stress and poor sleep
  • Age (metabolism slows with age)

Healthy Weight Management

Nutrition

  • Eat more vegetables and fruits
  • Choose lean proteins
  • Pick whole grains over refined grains
  • Limit sugary drinks and snacks
  • Watch portion sizes
  • Eat slowly and mindfully
  • Limit restaurant and fast food

Physical Activity

  • Aim for 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week
  • Include strength training 2 days per week
  • Find activities you enjoy
  • Start slowly and build up

Lifestyle

  • Get 7-9 hours of sleep
  • Manage stress
  • Limit alcohol
  • Don't skip meals
  • Drink plenty of water

Realistic Weight Loss Goals

Healthy weight loss is 1-2 pounds per week. To lose 1 pound, you need to cut 3,500 calories.

Better approach:

  • Focus on health improvements, not just weight
  • Set small, achievable goals
  • Track progress in multiple ways (energy, clothes fit, measurements)
  • Expect plateaus - this is normal
  • Focus on habits, not just numbers

When to Seek Help

  • Your BMI is 30 or higher
  • You have weight-related health problems
  • You've tried diet and exercise without success
  • You want personalized weight management

Options include:

  • Talk to your doctor
  • See a registered dietitian
  • Join a weight management program
  • Consider medication or surgery if recommended by doctor

Avoiding Unhealthy Diets

Be cautious of:

  • Crash diets (lose weight too fast)
  • Extreme restriction (not sustainable)
  • Diets that eliminate entire food groups
  • Diet pills or supplements (often dangerous)
  • Very low-calorie diets (can harm metabolism)

Best approach: Small, sustainable changes you can maintain for life.

Body Positivity

While managing weight is important, remember:

  • You are worthy at any size
  • Health comes in different shapes and sizes
  • Be kind to your body
  • Focus on how you feel, not just how you look
  • Avoid comparison to others
