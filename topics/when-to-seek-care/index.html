When to Seek Medical Care

Knowing When to Get Help

It can be hard to know when a health problem is serious. These guidelines can help you decide when to call your doctor or seek emergency care.

Call 911 or Go to the ER If You Have:

  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Severe bleeding
  • Sudden weakness or numbness
  • Confusion or trouble speaking
  • Severe allergic reaction
  • High fever that won’t go down

Call Your Doctor If You Notice:

  • New or worsening symptoms
  • Side effects from medications
  • Changes in blood pressure or blood sugar
  • Persistent pain
  • Swelling in legs or feet
  • Unexplained weight changes

When to Seek Care for Chronic Conditions

  • Your usual treatments aren’t working
  • You feel dizzy, weak, or faint
  • Your symptoms interfere with daily life
  • You have trouble breathing during normal activities

Mental Health: When to Reach Out

  • Feeling hopeless or overwhelmed
  • Loss of interest in activities
  • Thoughts of self‑harm
  • Severe anxiety or panic attacks

Help is available. Talk to a doctor, counselor, or trusted person.

Preparing for a Doctor Visit

  • Write down your symptoms
  • Bring a list of medications
  • Note when symptoms started
  • Bring recent vital sign readings
