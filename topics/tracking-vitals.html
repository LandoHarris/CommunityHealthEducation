Why Track Your Vitals?

Tracking vital signs at home helps:

  • Monitor your health between doctor visits
  • Catch problems early
  • See if treatments are working
  • Provide information to share with your doctor
  • Help manage chronic conditions
  • Identify patterns and triggers

What Are Vital Signs?

Vital signs are measurements that show how your body is working:

  • Blood pressure
  • Heart rate (pulse)
  • Temperature
  • Breathing rate
  • Blood sugar (glucose)
  • Weight

Blood Pressure

How to Check at Home

  • Buy a home blood pressure monitor (automatic or manual)
  • Sit comfortably for 5 minutes before measuring
  • Rest your arm at heart level
  • Place the cuff snugly on bare arm
  • Check at same time each day (usually morning)
  • Take multiple readings for accuracy

What to Record

  • Date and time
  • Systolic and diastolic numbers
  • Arm used (left or right)
  • How you felt (stressed, calm, etc.)

Heart Rate (Pulse)

Normal resting heart rate: 60-100 beats per minute

How to Check Your Pulse

  1. Find your pulse on your wrist (inside, near thumb)
  2. Or find it on your neck (gentle pressure on side)
  3. Use two fingers (index and middle)
  4. Count beats for 60 seconds
  5. Or count for 15 seconds and multiply by 4

If Your Resting Heart Rate Is:

  • Below 60: Talk to your doctor
  • 60-100: Normal
  • Above 100: Call your doctor (may indicate stress, illness, or heart problem)

Temperature

Normal body temperature: 98.6°F (37°C)

Taking Your Temperature

  • Digital thermometer (easiest): Under tongue, 1 minute
  • Ear thermometer: In ear, 2-3 seconds
  • Temporal thermometer: Across forehead
  • Use same thermometer and location each time

Temperature Guide

  • Below 95°F: Too low - call doctor
  • 98.6°F ± 1°: Normal
  • 99-100.4°F: Slight fever
  • 100.4°F or higher: Fever - contact doctor
  • 103°F or higher: High fever - call doctor or go to ER

Blood Sugar (Glucose)

If you have diabetes, your doctor will tell you how often to check.

How to Check at Home

  1. Use a glucose meter (provided by doctor)
  2. Wash hands and prick finger
  3. Place blood on test strip
  4. Insert strip in meter
  5. Read number (results in seconds)

Target Ranges (vary by person)

  • Fasting (before eating): 70-130 mg/dL
  • 2 hours after meals: Less than 180 mg/dL
  • Ask your doctor for YOUR target range

Weight

Tips for Tracking Weight

  • Weigh at same time each day (usually morning)
  • Use the same scale
  • Weigh yourself in similar clothing
  • Track weekly (daily can be discouraging)
  • Look at trends over weeks, not daily changes

Keeping Records

Good records help your doctor care for you:

  • Use a notebook or app
  • Record date, time, and measurement
  • Note anything unusual (illness, stress, exercise, diet)
  • Bring records to doctor visits
  • Look for patterns and trends

Home Monitoring Devices

Vital Sign Device Cost Range
Blood Pressure Automatic cuff $25-$60
Heart Rate Pulse oximeter or fitness band $20-$100
Temperature Digital thermometer $5-$30
Blood Sugar Glucose meter (provided by doctor) Free to $50
Weight Digital scale $20-$100

When to Contact Your Doctor

  • Readings are consistently high or low
  • You see sudden changes
  • You have symptoms with abnormal readings
  • You're unsure how to use your device
  • You want help interpreting your numbers
