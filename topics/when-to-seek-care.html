Call 911 for Emergencies

Don't wait. Go to the nearest emergency room or call 911 immediately if you experience life-threatening symptoms.

When to Call 911 or Go to the ER

Chest or Breathing Issues

  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Severe shortness of breath
  • Difficulty breathing at rest

Neurological Symptoms

  • Face drooping on one side
  • Arm weakness or numbness
  • Speech difficulty or slurring
  • Sudden severe headache
  • Confusion or difficulty understanding
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Severe dizziness

Severe Bleeding or Injury

  • Heavy bleeding that won't stop
  • Suspected broken bone
  • Severe burns
  • Trauma or serious accident

Other Emergencies

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or others
  • Severe allergic reaction (swelling, difficulty breathing)
  • Choking
  • Poisoning or overdose
  • Severe abdominal pain

When to Call Your Doctor

Call during office hours or use the after-hours line for:

  • Fever lasting more than 3 days
  • Cough lasting more than 2 weeks
  • Persistent vomiting or diarrhea
  • Severe pain that over-the-counter medication doesn't help
  • Difficulty urinating or painful urination
  • Unexplained rash or skin changes
  • Signs of infection (wound not healing, increasing redness)
  • Severe fatigue or weakness
  • New or worsening health symptoms
  • Questions about medications or side effects
  • Changes in appetite, weight, or sleep

When to Visit Urgent Care

Urgent care is for non-life-threatening problems that need prompt attention:

  • Moderate fever (101-103°F)
  • Mild to moderate pain
  • Sprains or strains
  • Minor cuts or burns needing stitches
  • Ear or sinus pain
  • Sore throat
  • Mild dehydration
  • Urinary tract infection symptoms
  • Mild allergic reactions
  • Minor head injury (without severe symptoms)

When to Schedule a Doctor's Appointment

These can wait for a regular appointment:

  • Routine check-ups and screenings
  • Medication refills
  • Chronic condition follow-ups
  • New symptoms lasting several days (not emergency)
  • Questions about health or lifestyle
  • Mental health concerns
  • Annual physicals

When Something is an Emergency

Use this guide:

  • Chest or severe pain: Emergency
  • Difficulty breathing: Emergency
  • Confusion or change in mental status: Emergency
  • Severe bleeding: Emergency
  • Thoughts of self-harm: Emergency (988)
  • Sudden vision loss: Emergency
  • Severe headache: Emergency
  • When in doubt: Call 911 or go to ER
Don't Wait When in Doubt

It's better to go to the emergency room and be told you're okay than to ignore warning signs. Hospitals would rather evaluate you and find out it's not serious than miss a real emergency.

How to Prepare for Medical Visits

  • Keep a list of current medications and dosages
  • Write down your symptoms (when they started, what makes them worse)
  • Write down questions to ask your doctor
  • Bring recent medical records or test results
  • Know your allergies and medications you've reacted to
  • Write down family medical history
  • Arrive early to allow time for paperwork

Questions to Ask Your Doctor

  • What is causing my symptoms?
  • What treatment do you recommend?
  • Are there other options?
  • What are the side effects?
  • When will I feel better?
  • Do I need any tests?
  • When should I follow up?
  • What should I do if symptoms don't improve?
  • How do I take medications (with food, etc.)?
  • Is it safe to exercise?

Finding Healthcare

  • Don't have a doctor? Call 211 to find community health centers
  • Can't afford care? Ask about sliding scale fees
  • Need a specialist? Ask your doctor for a referral
  • Insurance questions? Call your insurance company or visit healthcare.gov
  • Urgent care: Check locations and hours online
