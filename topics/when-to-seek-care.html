When to Seek Medical Care

Call 911 for Emergencies

If symptoms are life-threatening, call 911 immediately.

When to Call 911 or Go to the ER

Chest or Breathing Issues

  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Severe shortness of breath
  • Difficulty breathing at rest

Stroke Symptoms

  • Face drooping
  • Arm weakness
  • Speech difficulty
  • Sudden severe headache
  • Confusion or trouble understanding

Severe Injury or Bleeding

  • Heavy bleeding
  • Suspected broken bone
  • Severe burns
  • Major accident or trauma

Other Emergencies

  • Thoughts of self-harm
  • Severe allergic reaction
  • Choking
  • Poisoning or overdose
  • Severe abdominal pain

When to Call Your Doctor

Call during office hours or use after-hours line for:

  • Fever lasting more than 3 days
  • Cough lasting more than 2 weeks
  • Persistent vomiting or diarrhea
  • Severe pain not relieved by medication
  • Painful urination
  • Unexplained rash
  • Signs of infection
  • Severe fatigue
  • New or worsening symptoms
  • Medication questions

When to Visit Urgent Care

Urgent care is for non-life-threatening issues:

  • Moderate fever (101–103°F)
  • Sprains or strains
  • Minor cuts needing stitches
  • Ear or sinus pain
  • Sore throat
  • Mild dehydration
  • UTI symptoms
  • Mild allergic reactions

When to Schedule a Regular Appointment

  • Routine check-ups
  • Medication refills
  • Chronic condition follow-ups
  • New symptoms lasting several days
  • Mental health concerns
  • Annual physicals
Don't Wait When in Doubt

It's better to get checked and be safe than ignore warning signs.

How to Prepare for Medical Visits

  • List your medications
  • Write down symptoms and when they started
  • Bring questions
  • Bring medical records if available
  • Know your allergies
  • Arrive early

Questions to Ask Your Doctor

  • What is causing my symptoms?
  • What treatment do you recommend?
  • Are there alternatives?
  • What are the side effects?
  • Do I need tests?
  • When should I follow up?

Finding Healthcare

  • No doctor? Call 211 for local clinics
  • Low cost care: Ask About.html sliding scale fees
  • Need a specialist? Request a referral
  • Insurance questions? Contact your insurer
