Why Supporting Others Matters

When someone you care about is ill or struggling with a health challenge, your support can make a real difference in their recovery and well-being.

Social connection and emotional support are important for health.

How to Listen Effectively

  • Really listen: Put away phones and distractions
  • Don't interrupt: Let them finish speaking
  • Validate feelings: Say things like "That sounds really hard"
  • Don't minimize: Avoid saying "at least..." or "others have it worse"
  • Ask open-ended questions: "How are you feeling?" not "Are you okay?"
  • Avoid advice unless asked: Just listening can be enough
  • Be present: Sitting together in silence is okay

Practical Ways to Help

Daily Needs

  • Provide meals or groceries
  • Help with household tasks (cleaning, laundry)
  • Run errands (pharmacy, appointments)
  • Take them to doctor's appointments
  • Help with childcare or pet care

Emotional Support

  • Visit or call regularly
  • Send cards or messages
  • Attend medical appointments if invited
  • Include them in activities if they're able
  • Sit with them during difficult times

Information & Advocacy

  • Help research their condition (if they want)
  • Help organize medical records
  • Accompany them to appointments to help listen
  • Help them advocate for their care
  • Connect them with support groups or resources

What Not to Do

  • Don't take over - let them maintain independence when possible
  • Don't force optimism - "stay positive" isn't always helpful
  • Don't share your own medical stories unless asked
  • Don't disappear when things get hard
  • Don't assume you know what they need - ask
  • Don't gossip about their illness
  • Don't take it personally if they need space

Supporting Someone with Chronic Illness

  • Understand that some days are harder than others
  • Don't expect them to "look sick"
  • Support their treatment decisions
  • Help them manage multiple appointments
  • Celebrate small victories
  • Be patient with limitations
  • Stay consistently present over time

Supporting Someone with Mental Illness

  • Don't dismiss their symptoms
  • Encourage them to seek professional help
  • Let them know you care, even if they seem distant
  • Don't blame them for their illness
  • Be alert to signs of crisis
  • Know crisis resources (988 Suicide Prevention Lifeline)
  • Take threats of self-harm seriously

Supporting Someone in Grief

  • Let them grieve at their own pace
  • Don't rush them or tell them "they should be over it"
  • Share memories of the person who died
  • Check in often, especially on hard days
  • Help with practical tasks (errands, meals)
  • Acknowledge that grief doesn't end
  • Connect them with grief support groups if wanted

Taking Care of Yourself

While supporting others, remember to care for yourself:

  • Set boundaries - you can't help if you burn out
  • Take breaks when needed
  • Ask for help from other people
  • Maintain your own health and relationships
  • Seek counseling or support groups for caregivers if needed
  • Know that you can't fix everything - that's okay

When to Seek Professional Help

Encourage professional support if someone is:

  • Thinking about harming themselves
  • Expressing suicidal thoughts
  • Unable to care for themselves
  • Isolating completely
  • In crisis

Crisis Resources:

  • National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988
  • Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
  • Call 911 for immediate danger

Resources for Caregivers

  • Family Caregiver Alliance
  • AARP Caregiving Resources
  • Local support groups for caregivers
  • Local mental health and support services
Remember: Supporting someone means showing up, listening, and caring - not having all the answers. Your presence matters.
