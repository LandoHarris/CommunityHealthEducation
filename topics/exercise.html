Why Exercise Matters

Regular physical activity:

  • Strengthens your heart and lungs
  • Helps control weight
  • Lowers blood pressure and blood sugar
  • Improves mood and mental health
  • Reduces risk of disease
  • Improves balance and flexibility
  • Keeps muscles and bones strong

How Much Exercise Do You Need?

Adults (18-64 years)

  • Aerobic activity: 150 minutes of moderate activity per week (like brisk walking)
  • OR: 75 minutes of vigorous activity per week (like jogging)
  • Strength training: 2 days per week (all major muscle groups)

Older Adults (65+ years)

  • Aerobic activity: 150 minutes of moderate activity per week
  • Strength training: 2 days per week
  • Balance exercises: 3+ days per week (to prevent falls)

Types of Exercise

Aerobic (Cardio)

Gets your heart pumping and breathing faster:

  • Brisk walking
  • Jogging or running
  • Dancing
  • Swimming or water aerobics
  • Cycling
  • Playing sports

Strength Training

Builds and maintains muscle:

  • Weight lifting
  • Resistance bands
  • Push-ups and sit-ups
  • Yoga
  • Gardening (heavy work)

Flexibility

Maintains range of motion:

  • Stretching
  • Yoga
  • Tai chi
  • Pilates

Balance

Prevents falls:

  • Tai chi
  • Balance exercises (stand on one leg)
  • Walking on a line
  • Yoga

Getting Started

  • Talk to your doctor first - Especially if you have health conditions
  • Start slowly - Begin with 10-15 minutes per day
  • Build gradually - Increase by 10% each week
  • Choose activities you enjoy - You're more likely to stick with them
  • Find a buddy - Exercise is more fun with a friend
  • Set realistic goals - Small steps lead to big results

Easy Ways to Add Activity

You don't need a gym. Try these:

  • Walk instead of driving short distances
  • Garden or yard work
  • Take stairs instead of elevators
  • Do housework
  • Dance to your favorite music
  • Play with grandchildren
  • Go for a walk after meals
  • Join a community class or group

Safe Exercise Tips

  • Warm up for 5-10 minutes before exercising
  • Cool down and stretch afterwards
  • Wear comfortable, supportive shoes
  • Stay hydrated - drink water before, during, and after
  • Listen to your body - stop if you feel pain
  • Exercise in safe, well-lit areas
  • Tell someone where you're going

When to Stop and Rest

Stop exercising and contact your doctor if you experience:

  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Severe shortness of breath
  • Dizziness or fainting
  • Severe joint or muscle pain
