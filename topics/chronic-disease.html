Chronic Disease Management

What is a Chronic Disease?

A chronic disease is a health condition that lasts a long time (usually 3 months or more). It develops slowly and cannot be caught from other people.

Chronic diseases are common in rural Alabama. Many people have one or more.

Common Chronic Diseases

  • Diabetes
  • Heart disease
  • High blood pressure
  • Arthritis
  • COPD (lung disease)
  • Asthma
  • Kidney disease
  • Cancer
  • Depression and anxiety

Living with Chronic Disease

You can manage chronic disease and live a full life:

  • Take your medications as prescribed
  • Monitor your condition (check blood sugar, blood pressure, etc.)
  • Follow your doctor's advice
  • Exercise regularly
  • Eat a healthy diet
  • Manage stress
  • Get enough sleep
  • Avoid smoking and excess alcohol

Managing Your Medications

  • Take medications exactly as prescribed
  • Use a pill organizer or reminder app
  • Keep a list of all medications
  • Tell your doctor about side effects
  • Get refills before you run out
  • Store medications properly

Working with Your Doctor

  • See your doctor regularly
  • Discuss your symptoms and concerns
  • Ask questions you don't understand
  • Get vaccinations recommended for you
  • Report new symptoms right away
  • Keep medical records

Preventing Complications

Good management prevents serious complications:

  • Monitor your condition regularly
  • Catch problems early
  • Follow treatment plans
  • Make lifestyle changes
  • Stay informed about your disease
  • Join support groups

Mental Health & Chronic Disease

Living with chronic disease can affect your mental health. It's normal to feel:

  • Frustrated
  • Worried
  • Depressed
  • Isolated

Talk to your doctor or a therapist. Depression and anxiety are treatable.

When to Seek Help

  • Your symptoms are getting worse
  • You're having side effects from medications
  • You don't understand your treatment
  • You're struggling emotionally
  • You have new or unusual symptoms
