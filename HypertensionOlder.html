Hypertension in Older Adults

Ages 65+: Managing blood pressure for healthy aging.

Overview

Hypertension is extremely common among older adults. More than two-thirds of people over age 65 have high blood pressure. As we age, blood vessels naturally become stiffer and less flexible, making blood pressure harder to control.

Managing hypertension is essential to reduce the risk of heart attack, stroke, kidney disease, and cognitive decline, while helping maintain independence and quality of life.

Unique Challenges for Older Adults

  • Age-related changes in blood vessels and heart function
  • Multiple chronic conditions (diabetes, kidney disease, etc.)
  • Polypharmacy (taking multiple medications)
  • Increased sensitivity to medication side effects
  • Risk of dizziness, falls, or blood pressure fluctuations

These factors can make hypertension more complex to manage and require careful monitoring and individualized care.

Management Strategies

  • Monitor blood pressure regularly at home and during visits
  • Take medications exactly as prescribed
  • Follow a heart-healthy diet (low salt, rich in fruits and vegetables)
  • Stay physically active with safe, appropriate exercises
  • Limit alcohol and avoid tobacco
  • Manage other chronic conditions (diabetes, cholesterol, etc.)
  • Report any side effects or symptoms promptly

Why It Matters

Good blood pressure control can significantly reduce the risk of heart attacks, strokes, kidney failure, and memory problems. It also helps maintain independence and daily function.

Even small improvements in blood pressure can lead to meaningful long-term health benefits.

Stay Healthy as You Age

Work toward better blood pressure control to support a healthier, more independent life.
