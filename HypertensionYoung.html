Hypertension in Young Adults

Ages 18–39: Early awareness for lifelong heart health.

Overview

Hypertension can affect young adults, even though it is often overlooked in this age group. About 1 in 5 young adults in the U.S. have high blood pressure, but many are unaware of their condition.

Early detection and healthy habits can prevent long-term complications and set the foundation for a healthy future.

Risk Factors in Young Adults

  • Family history of hypertension
  • Overweight or obesity
  • Poor diet (high in salt, low in fruits and vegetables)
  • Physical inactivity
  • Smoking or vaping
  • Excessive alcohol use
  • Chronic stress

Many risk factors are related to lifestyle choices, making this a key time for prevention.

Why Early Prevention Matters

High blood pressure in young adulthood can silently damage blood vessels and organs over time, increasing the risk of heart disease, stroke, and kidney problems later in life.

The earlier healthy habits are adopted, the greater the long-term benefits.

Prevention Tips for Young Adults

  • Check your blood pressure regularly—even if you feel healthy
  • Maintain a healthy weight
  • Eat a balanced, low-salt diet
  • Be physically active most days of the week
  • Avoid tobacco and limit alcohol
  • Manage stress with healthy coping strategies
