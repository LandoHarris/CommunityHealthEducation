Hypertension in Older Adults

Ages 65+: Managing blood pressure for healthy aging.

Overview

Hypertension is extremely common among older adults. More than two-thirds of people over age 65 have high blood pressure. As we age, blood vessels naturally become stiffer and less flexible, making blood pressure harder to control.

Managing Hypertension is essential to reduce the risk of heart attack, stroke, kidney disease, and cognitive decline, while helping maintain independence and quality of life.

Unique Challenges for Older Adults

  • Age-related changes in blood vessels and heart function
  • Multiple chronic conditions (e.g., diabetes, kidney disease)
  • Polypharmacy (managing multiple medications)
  • Increased sensitivity to medication side effects
  • Risk of orthostatic hypotension (dizziness or lightheadedness when standing up)

These factors make Hypertension more complex to manage and require close collaboration with healthcare providers.

Management Strategies

  • Regular Monitoring: Track blood pressure at home to help your doctor adjust medications safely.
  • Medication Adherence: Take medications exactly as prescribed; report any dizziness or falls to your doctor immediately.
  • Heart-Healthy Nutrition: Follow low-sodium guidelines and focus on nutrient-dense foods.
  • Safe Activity: Engage in regular, physician-approved movement (e.g., walking, water aerobics).
  • Coordinated Care: Ensure your healthcare provider is aware of all medications and supplements you are taking.

Why It Matters

Good blood pressure control can significantly reduce the risk of heart attacks, strokes, kidney failure, and memory problems. It is a vital component of maintaining independence and daily function.

Even small, sustained improvements in blood pressure can lead to meaningful long-term health benefits.

Work toward better blood pressure control to support a healthier, more independent life.
