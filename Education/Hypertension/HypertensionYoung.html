Hypertension in Young Adults

Ages 18–39: Early awareness and prevention are key.

Overview

Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is not just a concern for older adults. About 1 in 5 young adults in the U.S. already have elevated blood pressure, often without knowing it. Early detection and healthy habits can prevent long-term health problems.

Many young adults feel healthy and may not see a doctor regularly, making it easy for high blood pressure to go unnoticed. However, the effects of uncontrolled hypertension can begin early and last a lifetime.

Risk Factors in Young Adults

  • Family history of hypertension
  • Overweight or obesity
  • Poor diet (high in salt, low in fruits/vegetables)
  • Physical inactivity
  • Smoking or vaping
  • Excessive alcohol use
  • Chronic stress

Even without symptoms, these factors can increase the risk of developing high blood pressure at a young age.

Why Early Prevention Matters

High blood pressure in young adulthood can damage blood vessels and organs over time, leading to heart disease, stroke, and kidney problems later in life. The earlier you start healthy habits, the more you can reduce your risk.

Regular blood pressure checks—even if you feel fine—are important for early detection and prevention.

Prevention Tips for Young Adults

  • Stay physically active—aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week
  • Eat a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains
  • Limit salt and processed foods
  • Avoid tobacco and limit alcohol
  • Manage stress with healthy coping strategies
  • Get regular blood pressure checks

Take Charge of Your Health Early

Small changes now can prevent serious health problems later.
