Hypertension in Young Adults

Ages 18–39: Early awareness for lifelong heart health.

Overview

Hypertension can affect young adults, even though it is often overlooked. About 1 in 5 young adults in the U.S. have high blood pressure, but many are unaware because it rarely causes noticeable symptoms.

Early detection and healthy habits can prevent long-term complications and set the foundation for a healthy future.

Risk Factors in Young Adults

  • Family history of hypertension
  • Overweight or obesity
  • Poor diet (high in processed sodium, low in fruits and vegetables)
  • Physical inactivity
  • Smoking, vaping, and tobacco use
  • Excessive alcohol use
  • Chronic stress

Why Early Prevention Matters

High blood pressure in young adulthood can silently damage your blood vessels and organs over time. Think of it as "vascular wear and tear"—preventing it now ensures your heart and arteries remain flexible and healthy for decades to come.

The earlier healthy habits are adopted, the greater the long-term benefits for your cardiovascular system.

Prevention Tips for Young Adults

  • Know your numbers: If your blood pressure is normal, aim to have it screened at least every 3–5 years.
  • Maintain a healthy weight: Focus on sustainable, long-term habits.
  • Eat a balanced diet: Prioritize whole foods over processed, high-sodium choices.
  • Stay active: Get at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week.
  • Avoid nicotine: This includes cigarettes, e-cigarettes, and vapes.
  • Manage stress: Develop healthy coping mechanisms early.

Start Healthy Habits Now

Small changes today can protect your heart for years to come.
