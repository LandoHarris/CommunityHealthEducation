Preventing Hypertension

Simple, everyday habits can help keep your blood pressure in a healthy range.

Overview

Hypertension is one of the most preventable health conditions. While some risk factors like age and genetics cannot be changed, many of the most important causes are related to lifestyle.

Taking steps early can prevent high blood pressure from developing. Healthy habits are the foundation of long-term heart health.

Healthy Lifestyle Habits

  • Maintain a healthy weight: Even small amounts of weight loss can lower blood pressure.
  • Be physically active: Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week.
  • Follow the DASH Diet: Focus on fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins.
  • Reduce sodium: Aim for less than 2,300 mg per day; 1,500 mg is ideal for many adults.
  • Limit alcohol and tobacco: Both can significantly raise blood pressure levels.
  • Prioritize sleep and stress management: Chronic stress and poor sleep contribute to long-term cardiac strain.

Heart-Healthy Eating

The DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) eating plan is proven to lower blood pressure.

  • Prioritize fresh, whole foods over processed, pre-packaged items.
  • Read nutrition labels to identify "hidden" sodium.
  • Increase potassium-rich foods (e.g., bananas, spinach, beans) to help the body flush out excess sodium.

Regular Monitoring

Because hypertension is often asymptomatic, monitoring is your best defense.

Pro-tip for accurate home readings: Sit quietly for 5 minutes before measuring, keep your feet flat on the floor, and support your arm at heart level. Consistent monitoring at the same time each day provides the most useful data for your healthcare provider.

Start Today

Small, consistent changes lead to big improvements in your health over time.
